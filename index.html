Eco Era

This forest has stood for centuries, now its future rests with us.

For 35 years, Eco Era has helped protect more than 10,000 acres of protected and co-stewarded rainforest at Cerro Nara, preserving its extraordinary biodiversity and life-sustaining watershed while offering nature-based wellness programs for generations to come.

What's at stake

Ten thousand acres, still connected.

Eco Era stewards 1,100 acres within a protected landscape of more than ten thousand. A rare stretch of land that remains whole, where forests, waterways, and wildlife move across boundaries. Once fragmented, these connections are difficult to restore.

250 m – 1,100 m

The ridge to the river

The reserve rises from the Naranjo River at 250 meters to a ridgeline above 1,100. Across that gradient are primary and secondary rainforest, headwater streams, waterfalls, and forest corridors within the Paso de la Danta and UNESCO's Savegre Biosphere Reserve.

The water

Five communities drink water that starts in this watershed. Tree roots intercept rainfall, slow it down, filter sediment, and push it into the karst limestone aquifer. Clear the slopes and you lose that: faster runoff, more erosion, and degraded water quality for everyone downstream.

Five chapters from the research on this land

The landscape
01

The landscape

850 vertical meters, from the Naranjo to the highest ridge

The Eco Era reserve climbs from roughly 250 meters above sea level to around 1,100 meters.

The climb passes through primary and secondary rainforest, steep ravines, headwater streams, mature canopy, waterfalls, and a succession of habitats shaped by elevation and water.

The Naranjo at the lower boundary

Sandy banks and natural pools along the Naranjo. Otters, glass frogs and the amphibians that hold the streams together all depend on the riparian trees keeping this edge shaded and stable.

Steep ground and headwater streams

Ravines cut through and tributaries gather. Slopes pass 60 degrees in places, and mature root systems are the only thing holding them through a tropical storm.

Mature primary forest

Old growth, buttressed trunks, epiphytes, and a soil community built over centuries. This band carries the most carbon and the most structural complexity on the property.

The upper ridge

Cooler and wetter, with different plant communities. Birds arrive here seasonally from lower ground, which only works while the whole gradient stays forested.

The architecture beneath the forest

The beauty of Cerro Nara is inseparable from its geology. The country here is steep and structurally active, shaped by tectonic uplift, erosion, intense rainfall, and the constant movement of water downhill.

Roots bind the soil, leaf litter softens the force of rainfall, and understory growth slows water moving across the surface, while fallen branches, fungi, soil organisms and decaying matter keep building the porous ground beneath the canopy.

The water
02

The water

What the forest does to rain

Stand beneath this canopy during heavy rain and surprisingly little water reaches the ground at once.

A standing forest is part of the watershed's infrastructure.

Leaves catch it

Leaves catch it and branches redirect it, so it travels slowly down trunks and through layers of vegetation before touching soil.

Belowground

Belowground, roots open pathways through the earth. Organic matter acts like a sponge. Water soaks in instead of running straight downhill.

The delay

That delay reduces erosion, keeps sediment out of streams, recharges groundwater, and keeps streams running after the rain has passed.

Clean water should be something you can test

Water is sampled and recorded at points across the property, from the ridge to the lower boundary. Scientific testing across the region found pH, nitrates, chlorides, and alkalinity all within healthy natural ranges and safely below drinking water limits.

These ecosystems create clean drinking water.

More than half of the Llano Grande watershed lies within the Eco Era reserve forest. From these mountains, water flows into the Naranjo and Savegre river systems, two of the cleanest river networks in Central America. Tree roots slow rainfall, stabilize soil, and feed water deep into the limestone aquifer that supplies drinking water to communities including Londres, Naranjito, and Villanueva.

If these forests are cleared, the watershed changes immediately. Sediment increases, aquifers weaken, and water quality declines downstream.

What the tests tell us, in plain terms.

Water is sampled at streams from the ridge down to the river. Three simple tests show whether anything upstream, such as farming, cattle or clearing, is changing it.

pH measures how acidic or alkaline water is. Every stream tested sits close to neutral, the same zone as healthy drinking water. Why it matters: fish, frogs and insects can only live in a narrow range, and runoff or pollution pushes pH out of it.

Low nitrate

Nitrate comes mostly from fertilizer, animal waste and sewage. Levels here stayed low across the property.

Why it matters: high nitrate feeds algae that use up the oxygen streams need. Low readings mean little is draining in from farms or settlements.

Low conductivity

Conductivity shows how many salts and minerals are dissolved in the water. Readings match a watershed nobody has disturbed.

Why it matters: it rises quickly when soil erodes or waste enters a stream, so it works as an early warning.

The life
03

The life

Surveys repeated over years

A single survey records what researchers found in one window. Repeating it over years shows what is changing.

Species appear or disappear, populations shift, and change can be measured against what was here before. Eco Era works with academic researchers, including survey work associated with the University of Costa Rica.

Seed dispersal and forest regeneration

Bats, at 62 percent of mammal diversity here, disperse thousands of seeds every night across distances up to 10 km. Frugivorous birds carry seeds that wind cannot move. Tapirs and primates do more of the same. Pull any part of this out and the forest loses its ability to regenerate.

Predator and prey regulation

Jaguars, pumas, and ocelots are documented in this corridor. When apex predators are present, every level of the food web below them is functioning. Herbivores are kept in check, so plant communities keep their structure all the way down the food web.

Pollination networks

The orchid richness on this property, multiple miniature species and several endemic to the region, is a useful indicator. Orchids are among the most specialized plants in terms of their pollinator relationships. High orchid diversity means the pollinator community is genuinely complex and intact.

Altitudinal migration corridor

The property spans 250 to 1,100 meters, and that range matters. Three-wattled bellbirds, documented near Salitrillos, migrate vertically with the seasons, and other frugivores follow the fruiting season up and down the slope. They need intact forest at every elevation they pass through. This corridor gives them that.

Beyond the boundary
04

Beyond the boundary

The Paso de la Danta, or Path of the Tapir

A forest may be legally protected and still fail ecologically if it becomes isolated.

Without connected habitat, forests become isolated islands where species lose their migration routes and populations shrink.

The Paso de la Danta, or Path of the Tapir

Established in 2000, the Paso de la Danta Biological Corridor stretches across more than 82,000 hectares of southern Costa Rica, linking coastal rainforest with mountain cloud forest. Named after the endangered Baird's tapir, the corridor exists to keep wildlife moving through an increasingly fragmented landscape. The Eco Era reserve sits within one of the corridor's most critical sections.

Forest cover has recovered

Since the corridor was established, forest cover across the region has significantly recovered, showing that protection works when land stays connected.

Four million tonnes of carbon in standing forest

The primary forest on the Eco Era reserve property stores an estimated 4 million-plus tonnes of CO₂ at roughly 1,036.7 tonnes per hectare. For reference, the average American car emits about 4.6 tonnes a year, so the forest holds over 800,000 cars' annual emissions in wood, root systems, and decomposing matter.

The carbon is already stored in a standing forest, with no reforestation credit or promised future sequestration involved. If this land is cleared, that carbon is released.

The forest makes rain

Tree transpiration also recycles precipitation back into the atmosphere. Clearing the forest here reduces rainfall across the broader region.

Protection
05

Protection

Research should change how land is protected.

Species records show which habitat matters most, and water monitoring shows when the watershed's condition shifts.

Elevation data suggests where climate-sensitive species may move. Field observations send patrols to the edges under pressure.

Old-growth forest can't be replanted

What grows back in a decade, or even several, is a different ecological system from the one that was removed. The first task is often the least glamorous: keep what remains standing, through patrols, anti-poaching partnerships, and protection of Guanacaste, Manú, and Ocotea monteverdensis.

Protecting the upper watershed

Eco Era protects forest in the upper watersheds of the Naranjo and Savegre and maintains the vegetation, soils, tributaries, and riparian areas that keep them functioning, working with downstream communities.

Keeping routes open between properties

Direct land protection, partnerships with neighboring landowners, long-term species monitoring, anti-poaching efforts, and education that reduces conflict at the forest edge.

Bringing people into the forest

Conservation also needs people who know a place well enough to care what happens to it. Posada Natura is where many of them first get to know it.

Working toward permanent legal protection

One of Eco Era's most important priorities is strengthening the formal legal protection of the Eco Era reserve landscape through Costa Rica's conservation system and work with MINAE. Since 1991, the Caldarelli De Leon family has helped protect this landscape from the pressures that have fragmented tropical forests throughout the region.

Legal refuge status would protect the land regardless of who owns or manages it.

Posada Natura

When people know a place directly, when they walk beneath its canopy, swim in its rivers, learn its species, and begin to understand the systems that sustain life there, protection becomes personal.

Posada Natura is built for that kind of time. The retreat center offers a place for rest and unhurried time in the forest.

The connection runs both ways. The land offers people a place to return to themselves, and that experience can make people more willing to protect it.

The programs

Plant Medicine Retreats

Ceremonial programs draw from more than 15 years of facilitation and relationship with Colombian Indigenous traditions.

The work takes place within the living environment of Cerro Nara, where days are shaped by rain, the river and the dark, well away from ordinary routines.

Rainforest Integration

For some guests, the most important part of a transformative experience begins after the ceremony ends. Longer immersion programs create room for rest, meditation, movement, nature therapy, and integration before or after deeper retreat work.

Nature-Based Wellness

Forest bathing, river immersion, yoga, meditation, guided walks, and conservation education offer a quieter entry into the landscape.

First Responder and Veteran Programs

Firefighters, veterans, healthcare workers, and other people whose lives have required sustained exposure to stress carry experiences that are difficult to leave behind in ordinary settings.

Dedicated programs combine structured support with extended time in a natural environment removed from the rhythms of institutional and urban life. The intention is to create room for nervous-system regulation and recovery.

Repeated decisions, made across decades

1991

The decision

A collective of families commits to keeping the Eco Era reserve property intact, at a point when clearing and fragmentation were moving through most of the surrounding Central Pacific. Nothing about keeping it was inevitable.

Since

A wider landscape

Neighboring landowners join the conservation landscape. What began as one property becomes part of a connected block of more than 10,000 acres, held together by a shared mission to protect these ecosystems.

Ongoing

The surveys

Biological survey work starts recording what the forest contains, and water testing begins at the headwater streams. Repeated over years, those records become the baseline everything else is measured against.

Now

Toward permanence

The retreat center on the river now earns most of what protection costs. The remaining work is legal: moving the property toward permanent wildlife refuge status, so the forest outlasts the families who hold it.

Why Cerro Nara?

Because what remains here is getting rare: a large, connected rainforest that is still functioning as a living system.

The forest holds steep slopes in place, feeds the Naranjo and Savegre headwaters, stores carbon, and carries hundreds of documented species. Wildlife needs room to move, rivers need healthy headwaters, and forests need continuity across elevations as temperature and rainfall shift.

Fragment the landscape and those relationships start coming apart.

Primary rainforest also won't come back once it goes.

The complexity of an old forest is built over centuries out of soil, roots, fungi, water, canopy and the species working through all of it.
